About the Museum

Built to explain the brain twice: once for adults, once for children.

Each experience below carries a kid mode toggle, so the same idea can be handed to a curious child or to a colleague reviewing the science. Nothing here sits behind an email gate.

This is ongoing, working material rather than a finished body of knowledge — some experiences are further along than others, and neuroscience keeps moving underneath all of it. Licensed CC BY‑NC‑SA 4.0 and credited to Sharena Rice throughout.

The Experiences

Seven ways into the brain and body.

Each one stands alone — start wherever the topic pulls you.

Spatial navigation→

A Brief History of Inner Space

How your brain knows where you are: place cells, grid cells, head-direction cells, and speed cells, built into an interactive tour of your built-in GPS.
Illustrated & animated→

Reflex Arcs

Seven reflex circuits from knee-jerk to gag — the shortcuts your nervous system uses to keep you alive.
Molecular signaling→

The Chemistry of the Brain

How neurons talk to each other in molecules: dopamine, serotonin, glutamate, GABA, and the receptor twist that turns a signal into a feeling.
Rhythm & oscillation→

Waves of the Brain and Beyond

Delta, theta, alpha, beta, gamma, splines, and the rhythms beyond the cortex — how the brain keeps time with itself.
Plasticity→

The Adaptive Brain

Eighty-six billion neurons renegotiating their friendships every second: Hebb's rule, spike-timing plasticity, and a small trainable network.
Beyond the five→

Coming to More Senses

Nine senses that almost never get a name — vestibular, proprioception, interoception, nociception, and more.
Fifteen tools, seven approaches→

The Neuromodulation Experience

Electricity, magnetism, sound, light, touch, rhythm, and genetic switches — for clinicians and the patients they explain them to.
